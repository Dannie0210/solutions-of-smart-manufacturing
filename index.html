Project Scenario

We are acting as professional system architects, tasked by a demanding food-packaging customer to propose a credible inspection machine—and to defend its feasibility to an industry-experienced decision maker.

The customer commission

A reliable inspection concept for metal-lid glass jars

Our customer packages food in glass jars with metal lids. They want a system that assesses whether the closure meets its sealing requirements and removes inadequately sealed jars from the production line before they reach the next process.

“How can you verify sealing quality, handle different lid sizes, reject defective jars, and still make the solution commercially viable?”

Current Challenges

The current quality-control challenge is not just to measure a lid—it is to make the right sorting decision consistently, across multiple jar configurations, without weakening food-safety controls.

CHALLENGE 01 / QUALITY

Food-safety exposure

An improperly sealed lid can compromise product integrity. A false pass may allow an unsafe or suspect jar to continue downstream, making inspection sensitivity and reject reliability critical.

CHALLENGE 02 / COMPATIBILITY

Multiple lid diameters

The customer uses metal lids from 27 mm to 110 mm in diameter. Lid geometry, stiffness and deformation behavior can vary, so the same threshold may not work for every SKU.

CHALLENGE 03 / MANUFACTURING

Stable on-line decisions

Conveyor speed, jar stability, operator repeatability and reliable rejection all affect practical feasibility. The proposed design must align inspection, sorting and operating cost.

Important engineering distinction: Lid deflection and a manual “BOO” response are indirect indicators of closure condition, not direct proof of seal integrity. Both approaches require experiments with known-good and known-defective samples before production claims can be made.

Precision-oriented automation

Plan A — Laser Lid Inspection

AUTOMATED CONCEPT

Measure metal-lid displacement using laser triangulation; process the measurement to classify each jar; and activate a pneumatic pusher to divert a flagged jar into a reject area. The production line includes side guide rails to reduce tipping and an operator who supervises system operation.

Why this plan can meet the brief

A non-contact sensor and PLC-based reject logic offer a path to repeatable, traceable screening over multiple lid formats. Product-specific settings and actual defect-detection performance still need validation.

What must be demonstrated

Measurable height separation between good/bad seals; calibration by lid size; transport stability; false-pass/false-reject rates; pneumatic response timing; and capital/operating cost.

People-centered inspection

Plan B — Labor intelligence

MANUAL CONCEPT

Assign one student operator to each conveyor line. The operator presses a metal lid, listening or feeling for a characteristic “BOO” response; a flagged jar is manually pushed into the nearby reject area. The demo lets you add operators and conveyor lines (up to 22) and introduce good or defective jars.

The demo starts with two operators; adding a person also adds a production line and €400/month to stipend cost. At 22 operators, annual stipend cost is €105,600, leaving €4,400 before other expenses. Use the link above for a larger mobile view.

Why this plan may be attractive

It offers a simple conceptual process, visual quality checking and a labor-cost model that scales by operator count, without assuming an automated laser inspection machine is already available.

What must be demonstrated

Operator-to-operator consistency, acoustic/physical defect correlation, feasible jar throughput, fatigue effects, supervision, applicable internship rules and the true fully loaded cost.

Decision focus · Plan A

More automation and instrumentation, but higher technical-integration demands. Key proof: seal-classification performance across 27–110 mm lid sizes.

Decision focus · Plan B

Simple manual workflow with staffing expenses and productivity constraints. Key proof: repeatable detection and safe handling over a shift.
